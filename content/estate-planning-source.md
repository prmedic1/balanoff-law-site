## Core framing (from Dan's email)
The cornerstones of any solid estate plan are a Last Will and Testament, Powers of Attorney
for both Property and Healthcare, and a Revocable Living Trust. Together, these documents put
in writing how you want your assets distributed, who should make financial and healthcare
decisions on your behalf if you become incapacitated, and provide you with important tax and
probate avoidance benefits.

Estate planning is crucial for ensuring the management and distribution of your assets
according to your wishes after your death. A comprehensive estate plan can provide peace of
mind and protect your family's future.

## Research reference (10 essential documents)

### Last Will and Testament
The cornerstone of any estate plan. Per Illinois law, you must sign your will with two
witnesses present who are not beneficiaries. The will outlines how you want your assets
distributed upon death and allows you to: name beneficiaries for your property and assets;
appoint a guardian for minor children; select an executor to manage your estate. If the will
does not name an executor, the courts will appoint one.

### Living Trust
A living trust (revocable trust) allows you to protect and manage your assets for your
benefit while you are alive. Upon your death, the trust transfers your assets to the
designated beneficiaries without going through probate. Benefits: avoiding probate court,
maintaining privacy regarding your estate, potentially reducing estate taxes.

### Living Will
A living will (advance directive) clarifies your preferences for medical treatment if you
face terminal illness or incapacitation — whether you want life-sustaining treatments —
and helps guide your healthcare proxy and medical providers.

### Durable Power of Attorney
A durable power of attorney (DPOA) authorizes an agent to manage your financial and legal
affairs and remains in effect even if you become incapacitated.

### Healthcare Power of Attorney
Designates someone to make medical decisions for you if you cannot. A type of DPOA but
different in scope. Essential for ensuring your healthcare preferences are respected.

### HIPAA Release Form
Authorizes healthcare providers to share medical information with designated individuals so
your healthcare proxy and family can make informed decisions.

### Account Beneficiary Designations
Life insurance policies, retirement accounts, and payable-on-death (POD) accounts require
designated beneficiaries. Keep designations current and aligned with the overall plan.

### Guardianship Designations
If you have minor children, a guardianship designation in your will appoints someone you
trust as guardian. Can also designate a guardian for adult dependents with disabilities.

### Letter of Intent
Non-binding guidance for your executor and loved ones: funeral wishes, personal messages,
other instructions.

### Asset Inventory
A list of all assets — bank accounts, investments, real estate, personal property, debts and
liabilities — to help the executor locate and manage assets efficiently.
